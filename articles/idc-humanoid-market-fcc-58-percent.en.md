IDC published a forecast on 22 October estimating what the US import restrictions will do to robotics revenue. Its model puts the US humanoid market in 2030 fifty-eight percent below the trajectory projected before the bans.

Across the whole US robotics market, IDC puts cumulative lost revenue for 2026–2030 at more than $6 billion. More than $2 billion of that falls in 2030 alone. The compound annual growth of the categories in the model slows from about 13% to about 9%.

For humanoids the hit shows up in 2027: down 41% against the baseline. Household cleaning robots fare better — down 4% in 2027 and 18% by 2030.

## What was restricted

On 28 July 2026 the Federal Communications Commission added foreign-made advanced robots to its Covered List, the register of equipment restricted on national security grounds.

The rules are not an outright ban. Device models already authorised may still be imported, software and firmware updates are allowed at least until 1 January 2029, and applications for conditional approval of new devices are accepted until 1 January 2028. That is why the effect shows up as a slowdown rather than a collapse: new models miss the market and buyers postpone purchases.

Separately, on 22 October the FCC gave Intel a one-year waiver for foreign autonomous vehicles inside semiconductor plants. Intel applied on 16 October, citing protection of current production and the absence of US alternatives; such vehicles are usually made by Daifuku and Murata Machinery.

A Section 232 investigation into robots and industrial machinery has also been running since mid-2025, with measures ranging from tariffs to licences under discussion.

## An asymmetric market

The restrictions landed on a market that was already lopsided. In the first half of 2026 about 25,000 humanoids shipped worldwide — more than in all of 2025, when the figure was around 18,500. Roughly 95% of those shipments came from Chinese manufacturers, among them Unitree and AGIBOT.

IDC expects Chinese vendors to redirect supply to the UK, France, Germany and Eastern Europe. US developers — Tesla with Optimus, Figure AI, Agility, Apptronik — are still in pilots, while Unitree, UBTech, AgiBot and Fourier sell systems for under $20,000.

The result, in IDC's words, is a split into two industries with their own standards, suppliers and cost structures. US manufacturers lose access to Chinese supply chains and manufacturing scale; Chinese companies lose the US market. Rare earths, mostly mined and processed in China, add a second dependency.

## What it means

Fifty-eight percent sounds catastrophic, but it applies to a very small market. Twenty-five thousand machines in six months is an output the car industry produces in hours. A 58% cut in such a market says little about the technology and a lot about the fact that demand now depends on a regulator rather than on engineers.

For buyers the practical conclusion is smaller: under current rules existing equipment does not have to be replaced, but checking suppliers and robot models is worth the effort — the next restriction may land on their own fleet.

Italian company Generative Bionics has announced a Physical AI Data Factory: a 5,000 m² site where humanoids will perform industrial tasks while the system records everything that happens. It is due to be operational in the fourth quarter of 2027; the company is based in Genoa.

The plan is a network. After the first site in Italy, the company intends to open a second elsewhere in Europe and a third serving global markets. AMD is the infrastructure partner: the factory will run on its rack-scale Helios architecture, which is meant to process physical and synthetic data and run thousands of digital-twin variations in parallel.

## Why a factory instead of a dataset

Language models learned on text that the internet already held. Humanoids have no such corpus: data on how an object behaves under load, how a grasp changes when it slips, how a person steps aside, is only collected during real operations.

> "A robot is not only vision and is not only language. It is position, velocity, acceleration, temperature and contact forces, so there is a large variety of data you have to take into account."
>
> — Daniele Pucci, CEO of Generative Bionics

The factory is meant to close a real-to-sim-to-real loop. GENE.01 robots work in recreated industrial environments and generate data; every environment is duplicated as a digital twin where the same task is repeated across thousands of variations in layout, materials and conditions; models trained in simulation go back to the hardware for validation.

For GENE.01 the dataset includes vision, motion and signals from a full-body multimodal skin. The company is betting on touch and proximity sensing, not cameras alone.

## European sovereignty as the argument

Pucci explains the location with the industrial base rather than cheap land: over forty years Europe has built automated production, and that is a data source competitors overseas do not have.

> "The value that Europe has right now is within its industry, and we have to extract physical AI data in order to train our own models."
>
> — Daniele Pucci

Certification is the second argument. According to Pucci, many of the Chinese humanoids shown at trade fairs are not certified to enter industrial settings, and Generative Bionics deliberately goes B2B first, where safety and admission are settled before a sale.

## What to check

First, timing. Two years remain before the stated launch, and a factory at this scale needs more than money: it needs partners willing to hand over their own production areas for data capture. Second, the economics of an hour: the cost per operation has to be lower than the alternatives, or the data turns out golden. Third, who buys GENE.01 after the pilots — the company names no customers and no production volumes.

Data factories are not new in themselves: similar sites are being built in the US and China, and NVIDIA sells the simulation and compute underneath them. Generative Bionics differs in promising data, robots and models in one loop — and in selling that as the European alternative.
